## Sarcasm Identification Cheat Sheet

### 1. **Contradiction Between Statement and Reality**
- If a statement describes something as positive when it is obviously negative (or vice versa), it is likely sarcastic.
  - *Example*: "A wave of hypothermia and drownings will be a great way to start the year."
- If the praise or criticism does not match the described situation, suspect sarcasm.

### 2. **Exaggeration or Absurdity**
- Sarcasm often uses exaggeration to highlight the absurdity of a situation.
  - *Example*: "We should start a petition to ban breathing."
- If a suggestion or description is so extreme it cannot be taken literally, it is probably sarcastic.

### 3. **Irony: Saying the Opposite of What is Meant**
- If the literal meaning is the opposite of what would be expected or reasonable, it’s likely sarcastic.
  - *Example*: "Calling a whole electorate 'morons' is a great way to win them over."
- Look for statements that invert common sense or social norms.

### 4. **Mocking or Trivializing Serious Issues**
- Sarcasm can trivialize complex or serious issues by offering an oversimplified or dismissive solution.
  - *Example*: "Have you tried not being poor? It is much easier."
- If a statement makes a serious problem sound easy to solve, it’s probably sarcastic.

### 5. **Rhetorical Questions with Obvious Answers**
- Sarcastic statements often use rhetorical questions where the answer is obvious and the question is meant to mock.
  - *Example*: "How could Government control of the internet be bad?"

### 6. **Mismatch Between Cause and Effect**
- If the cause and effect in a statement don’t logically connect, it’s likely sarcastic.
  - *Example*: "He's over six feet, so he must be wonderful."
- Watch for statements that attribute unrelated positive or negative qualities.

### 7. **Praising Negative Behavior or Outcomes**
- If someone is praised for something that is clearly bad, it’s sarcasm.
  - *Example*: "He's such great relationship material when he's drunk."
- Similarly, if negative outcomes are described as "great," "fun," or "the best," it’s sarcasm.

### 8. **Obvious Falsehoods or Impossible Scenarios**
- If a statement describes something that is obviously false or impossible, it’s likely sarcastic.
  - *Example*: "Impossible, as there's no one on the internet."
- If the statement is factually absurd, suspect sarcasm.

### 9. **Use of "Of Course," "Obviously," "Clearly," etc.**
- These words can signal sarcasm when used to introduce an obviously false or exaggerated claim.
  - *Example*: "Of course there's no security cameras in the White House in the game, they said it's just like real life!"

### 10. **Contextual Clues**
- Consider the context: If the first part of a statement describes something negative, but the conclusion is positive (or vice versa), it’s likely sarcastic.
  - *Example*: "Every package I get from Amazon is late and busted up. So happy I pay for free shipping with Prime."

---

### **Quick Steps for the Test**
1. **Check for Contradiction:** Does the statement contradict reality or common sense?
2. **Look for Exaggeration:** Is the statement absurd or extreme?
3. **Assess Tone:** Is the praise or criticism mismatched with the context?
4. **Spot Rhetorical Questions:** Is the question mocking or has an obvious answer?
5. **Evaluate Cause and Effect:** Are unrelated things being linked?
6. **Watch for Mocking Solutions:** Is a complex issue being trivialized?
7. **Notice Impossible Claims:** Is the statement factually impossible?
8. **Check for Sarcastic Markers:** Words like "of course," "obviously," "clearly," etc.

---

**Remember:**  
- Sarcasm often says the opposite of what is meant, especially to mock, criticize, or highlight absurdity.
- If a statement makes you think, "No one would sincerely say that," it’s probably sarcastic.
