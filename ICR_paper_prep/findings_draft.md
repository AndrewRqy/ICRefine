# Findings

## 5.1 Overall Task Performance

We evaluate ICRefine (v3) against a one-shot Cheatsheet-ICL (CS-ICL) baseline across eleven BIG-Bench Hard tasks using reasoning-first (RF) mode throughout. The full pipeline achieves five wins, four losses, and two ties against CS-ICL (Table 1).

The strongest gains come from tasks where the pipeline's structured prior knowledge captures a formal or procedural algorithm that the training model can learn to apply reliably. **Formal_fallacies** (+6pp) and **web_of_lies** (+8pp) benefit most from Phase 1 PK: the ICR prior knowledge on formal_fallacies encodes a comprehensive propositional logic taxonomy (15 sections, implication direction rules, De Morgan's laws, contrapositive equivalence) paired with a 7-step evaluation strategy that disambiguates common invalid inference patterns. The web_of_lies prior knowledge encodes an anchor-and-propagate algorithm — a deterministic procedure that any model family can execute step-by-step.

Losses concentrate in tasks where the cheatsheet encodes model-specific reasoning traces rather than transferable rules. **Causal_judgement** falls −8.1pp below CS-ICL: the v3 CJ case study is a free-form narrative derived from a single training item's oracle reasoning, encoding gpt-4.1-mini's idiosyncratic treatment of joint causation. **Geometric_shapes** falls −3pp: Phase 2 generates case studies targeting vertex counting errors specific to the train model's SVG parsing failures, which do not generalize to the test distribution. **Snarks** falls −4.2pp: the two ACTIVATE IF case studies (``Context Mismatch Irony'' and ``Implausible Baseline Sarcasm'') match mini's pattern of failing on subtle irony, but impose mini's sarcasm-recognition heuristics on models that process irony differently.

Five tasks reach ceiling after Phase 0 alone (Bool, Nav, WOL, LD3, Sports ≥ 98%), confirming that ICR's primary value materializes on tasks with structured, learnable failure modes — not on tasks where the baseline already saturates.

## 5.2 Phase Contribution Analysis

Figure 1 decomposes accuracy by pipeline phase for gpt-4.1-mini across all 11 tasks (reasoning-first). Three distinct behavioral clusters emerge.

**Phase 0 saturates seven tasks.** Boolean_expressions, navigate, web_of_lies, logical_deduction_three, sports_understanding, formal_fallacies, and snarks all reach ≥ 95.8% test accuracy after the auto-bootstrap prior knowledge generation alone. For these tasks, the bootstrap LLM — seeded with 75 training examples — generates a prior knowledge section sufficient to reach or approach ceiling. No further patching or case study generation is needed; Phase 1 applies zero patches and Phase 2 yields zero case studies.

**Phase 1 PK patching drives gains on harder tasks.** On geometric_shapes, Phase 1 raises accuracy from 70.0% (Phase 0) to 77.0% by iteratively identifying failure modes and augmenting the SVG parsing algorithm. The v3 GS prior knowledge (nine sections, three tables) after Phase 1 covers M/L/A command semantics, polygon vertex counting with multi-subpath handling, quadrilateral disambiguation via dot-product angle verification, and arc classification by rotation tolerance — a substantially more complete algorithm than the Phase 0 bootstrap. Causal_judgement improves from Phase 0 (73.6%) through Phase 1 (71.3% in the PK-only ablation — note the slight drop reflects PK patching instability on this task).

**Phase 2 CS generation has asymmetric effects.** On geometric_shapes, Phase 2 reduces accuracy from 77.0% (PK-only) to 70.0% (full), a −7.0pp degradation that renders the final pipeline worse than the Phase 0 bootstrap on this task. On causal_judgement, Phase 2 contributes a marginally positive +2.3pp in the RF regime. The contrast reveals a central tension in Phase 2 design: case studies that reliably fix train-set partitions may not encode patterns that generalize beyond the training distribution.

The Phase-1-only ablation (experiment log: `runs/bbh_v3_phase1_only/`) confirms the GS finding on held-out data. Phase 1 only (no CS) achieves 54% on GS while v3 full achieves 48% — a −6pp case study penalty consistent across eval regimes.

## 5.3 Case Study Quality and the Partition Overfitting Problem

The core mechanism of Phase 2 is partition-based: the training set is partitioned by error type, and a case study is generated to fix each coherent failure cluster. This mechanism succeeds when failures are concentrated in reproducible error classes, and fails when they are dispersed.

**Geometric shapes exemplifies the design tension.** The two GS case studies generated by v3 target specific parse failures of gpt-4.1-mini:

- **CS1 (Correct Vertex Counting Across Multiple "M" Subpaths):** Activates when `has_multi_subpath = true`, `n_vertices ≈ 7`, and `error = miscounted_vertices`. The case study instructs the solver that multiple M commands may form one connected polygon and that endpoints across subpaths must be counted collectively.
- **CS2 (Arc Paths Misclassified as Circles Instead of Ellipses):** Activates when `has_arc = true` and `arc_radii_equal = true` with `rotation_angle significantly non-zero`. Instructs that equal radii alone do not classify an arc as a circle — the rotation angle must be near-zero.

Both case studies encode correct geometric rules. The problem is specificity: the ACTIVATE IF conditions are tuned to mini's exact failure modes (multi-subpath vertex count errors in specific size ranges, arc rotation confusion at specific tolerance thresholds). When other models fail on different geometric parsing errors — quadrilateral subtype disambiguation, distance-based shape classification — the case studies do not fire, and the overhead of processing them may interfere with the model's own geometric reasoning.

**Formal fallacies illustrates CS redundancy.** Four case studies were generated for FF, but three (CS1, CS2, CS4) all cover illicit conversion of conditionals (premise: A⇒B; conclusion: B⇒A). CS1 and CS4 are near-identical — same error type, same ACTIVATE IF conditions. This redundancy arises because Phase 2's greedy search encounters the illicit-conversion failure pattern repeatedly across partitions and generates a new case study each time without detecting the overlap. Only CS3 (invalid inference from a disjunction premise) covers orthogonal ground. A single, well-constructed illicit-conversion case study would likely be more effective than three near-duplicates that risk confusing models which already handle this inference correctly.

**The causal judgement failure is structural.** CJ has the most heterogeneous failure distribution: the 35 training failures span joint-AND causation, OR/redundant causes, prevention chains, norm violations, timing effects, and intentionality judgments. Phase 2 can only generate case studies for failure partitions — clusters of similar errors. When failures span 10+ scenario subtypes, no single partition is large enough to yield a high fix-rate case study without regressing others. The single CJ case study that did pass the gate (``Joint Causes: Garden Fertilizers'', fix_rate 42%) is a near-verbatim paraphrase of one training item's oracle reasoning — precisely the overfitting pattern we seek to avoid.

## 5.4 Oracle Contamination in Causal Reasoning

Experiment E3 tests whether Phase 2 oracle injection — providing the gold chain of thought (`item["reason"]`) during case study generation — caused the harmful CJ case study. We re-run the CJ pipeline with `--no-phase2-oracle`, disabling oracle context in the Phase 2 failure formatting.

**Phase 2 produces zero case studies without oracle.** With oracle removed, Phase 2 cannot generate a single non-regressing case study across all iterations and bins (experiment log: `runs/bbh_cj_no_p2oracle/`). The oracle context is not a helpful hint — it is a prerequisite for Phase 2 to function on CJ at all. Without it, the model cannot identify a correction rule that fixes the target partition without breaking others.

**Removing oracle improves all-model accuracy.** The E3 condition (Phase 1 only, no oracle) matches or beats v3 full (which added one oracle-contaminated CS) for all five evaluation models, with a striking +9.2pp improvement for gpt-4.1-mini on the train task and +3.5pp for gpt-4.1. The pattern is consistent: the single oracle-derived CJ case study was harmful to all models, and the no-oracle condition recovers this loss simply by not generating it.

This finding has a direct design implication: oracle injection enables case study generation on tasks where failure modes are not self-explanatory from incorrect predictions alone, but it also encodes oracle-level reasoning specificity that does not transfer. For tasks with heterogeneous failure distributions (CJ), the oracle contamination cost exceeds the benefit. For tasks with coherent, formal failure modes (GS arc classification), the oracle provides genuine structural guidance that results in transferable case studies.

## 5.5 Cheatsheet Transferability Across Models

To assess cross-model transferability, we evaluate the v3 cheatsheet (trained on gpt-4.1-mini) on four additional evaluation models: gpt-4.1, claude-3.7-sonnet, gemini-2.0-flash, and llama-3.3-70b. We report both the full cheatsheet condition and a PK-only ablation (Table 2; Fig. 2).

**PK-only is more transferable than the full cheatsheet on average.** Averaging over the five non-ceiling tasks (GS, FF, Snarks, DQ, CJ) where non-trivial cheatsheet content was generated, the full pipeline falls below CS-ICL for three of four non-train model families: GPT-4.1 (−4.0pp), Claude (−2.8pp), and Llama (−4.3pp). Only Gemini shows a positive full-pipeline transfer (+2.8pp). PK-only scores are generally closer to or above CS-ICL: Claude PK-only is +1.3pp above CS-ICL despite full pipeline being −2.8pp.

The gap between PK-only and full reflects case study harm. The case studies are generated from mini's training failures and encode mini's specific error patterns as ACTIVATE IF templates. These templates can interfere with other models' reasoning — a model that does not make the specific multi-subpath vertex error will still process the vertex-counting case study, potentially over-riding a correct solution. The prior knowledge, by contrast, encodes general rules (formal algorithms, procedural instructions, taxonomy tables) that any capable model can apply.

**The transferability pattern is task-structured.** WOL's case studies help all models in CoT mode (+10pp to +30pp CS contribution), because the WOL case studies (despite the partition key bug) encode structurally correct logical reasoning chains that amplify stepwise reasoning for all model families. CJ case studies hurt all models (−2.3pp consistently), regardless of model family — the oracle-contaminated narrative is universally harmful. GS case studies hurt the train model (−5pp) but help non-train models that happen to fail on the same geometric errors (+4pp to +11pp for claude/gemini/llama in verdict mode).

This task-level structure suggests that case study transferability depends primarily on whether the failure mode the CS targets is shared across models. Formal geometric parsing errors (multi-subpath, arc rotation) happen to be shared; mini's causal judgment errors (joint-AND causation in one specific scenario framing) are not.

## 5.6 Evolutionary Algorithm Phase 1 Extension

Standard Phase 1 applies sequential patching: each iteration generates a candidate PK revision targeting the most recent failure analysis, accepts or rejects it based on accuracy delta, and moves on. For tasks where the bootstrap PK already exceeds the accuracy threshold, sequential patching applies zero patches — the system stalls at the bootstrap quality ceiling.

We introduce an **Evolutionary Algorithm (EA) Phase 1** that replaces sequential patching with a population-based search: at each generation, three candidate PK rewrites are generated in parallel, scored on a held-out validation set (20% of training data), and the top two survivors are recombined into the next generation via crossover. Table 5 and Figure 5 compare EA vs. standard Phase 1 across the four non-ceiling tasks.

**EA produces meaningful gains on geometric_shapes (+6.0pp train accuracy).** Standard Phase 1 applies zero patches to GS — the bootstrap PK passes the fix-rate gate immediately and no further patching occurs. EA Phase 1 applies three patches, achieving 89.3% train accuracy and 0 case studies. The EA result eliminates the need for Phase 2 entirely on GS: the evolutionary search finds a PK variant that handles the vertex-counting and arc-classification failure modes directly in the prior knowledge, making the case studies redundant. This directly addresses the −7.0pp Phase 2 penalty observed in v3 — EA's improved PK makes Phase 2 unnecessary while achieving higher train accuracy than the standard pipeline's PK-only condition (89.3% vs. 83.3%).

**Snarks and disambiguation_qa also benefit.** EA Phase 1 improves snarks by +2.8pp (90.7% vs. 87.9%) with 1 case study retained, and DQ by +2.0pp (88.7% vs. 86.7%) with 3 case studies added after 1 patch. The EA's population-level search appears to find PK variants that are more specific to the actual failure modes, reducing the number of residual failures that Phase 2 must address.

**Causal judgement remains resistant.** EA Phase 1 adds zero patches on CJ and produces a 64.0% train accuracy — below the standard pipeline's Phase 1 result. The EA validation set (20% hold-out) is too small for CJ's heterogeneous failure distribution: any candidate PK that improves on the validation subset may worsen on the remainder. CJ's structural resistance to PK patching — whether sequential or evolutionary — confirms that this task's failure modes are not addressable through prior knowledge augmentation alone.

The EA extension suggests a practical deployment strategy: when sequential Phase 1 stalls at the bootstrap ceiling (zero patches applied), switching to EA can unlock further gains, particularly on tasks with coherent but hard-to-articulate failure modes that benefit from diverse candidate generation.

## 5.7 Cheatsheet Size Ablation (Preliminary)

We ablate two independent dimensions of cheatsheet size: the Phase 1 prior knowledge character limit (3K, 6K, 12K, unlimited) and the Phase 2 case study count cap (best-of-1, best-of-3, unlimited). We report train accuracy as a preliminary proxy, with test evaluation pending.

**Phase 1 PK size does not monotonically affect accuracy.** Table 4 shows no consistent ordering across the four PK char limits on the four ablation tasks. For geometric_shapes, 12K achieves the highest train accuracy (89.3%) while unlimited yields only 83.3% — suggesting that the size constraint itself can function as a regularizer, forcing the LLM to generate more concise and focused prior knowledge rather than exhaustive coverage. Formal_fallacies shows a similar pattern: 12K produces the highest accuracy (72.7%) with a 7K final PK, versus unlimited at 71.3% with an 8K PK. This counterintuitive finding suggests that brevity pressure at generation time may improve the signal-to-noise ratio in the prior knowledge, analogous to regularization in supervised learning.

**Phase 2 best-of-N selection improves CS quality for GS.** Table 3 compares CS count limits on the same tasks. For geometric_shapes, best-of-3 achieves 89.3% train accuracy versus unlimited's 83.3% — a +6pp improvement. The pool under best-of-N contains four candidates: one vertex-counting CS with a 100% fix rate (the strongest) and three arc-variant CS with 33% fix rates each. The best-of-3 selector retains the top-3 CS by fix rate (including the strong vertex-counting CS and two moderate arc CS), discarding the weakest arc variant. Best-of-1 achieves only 78.0% — dropping the moderate CS along with the weak ones loses coverage on the arc partition.

For snarks, best-of-1 achieves 91.6% — higher than both best-of-3 (87.9%) and unlimited (87.9%). This suggests that for snarks, a single, high-quality CS encoding the most common subtle irony pattern is superior to multiple CS that may introduce conflicting activation conditions. The best-of-N mechanism adapts naturally to both cases: on GS, a larger pool is beneficial (more diverse failure modes require more CS); on snarks, aggressive pruning produces the better result.

The best-of-N design includes an automatic threshold-loosening mechanism: when N is specified, the collection gate is relaxed from fix_rate ≥ 0.30 to fix_rate ≥ 0.20 (floor 0.15), allowing more candidates into the pool before post-hoc pruning. This decouples the collection threshold from the selection threshold — the collection gate controls pool diversity, the selection mechanism controls final quality.

## 5.8 Summary of Findings

ICRefine's three-phase pipeline delivers reliable gains on tasks with formal, procedural failure modes and encounters systematic difficulties on tasks requiring judgment or folk psychological reasoning. Our key findings are:

1. **Phase 0 bootstrap saturation is common.** Seven of eleven tasks reach ceiling after bootstrap alone, suggesting that the primary challenge in deploying ICR is not refinement but task selection.

2. **Phase 1 PK patching is the most transferable component.** PK-only conditions match or exceed full pipeline conditions for three of four non-train model families when averaged over non-ceiling tasks.

3. **Phase 2 case study generation introduces a model-signature transfer problem.** CS generated from train-model failures encode the specific error patterns of the train model (gpt-4.1-mini), which do not match the error distributions of other model families. The degree of harm correlates with failure heterogeneity: heterogeneous failures (CJ, oracle-contaminated) produce harmful CS; coherent geometric failures (GS multi-subpath, arc rotation) produce CS that hurt the train model but help models with the same failure mode.

4. **Oracle injection enables Phase 2 but causes contamination on judgment tasks.** Oracle context is necessary for CJ case study generation but produces a single training-item-specific narrative that degrades all-model accuracy by 2–8pp. Removing oracle produces zero CS and recovers the accuracy loss.

5. **EA Phase 1 unlocks gains where sequential patching stalls.** On GS, EA applies three PK patches and achieves +6pp above the standard pipeline, eliminating Phase 2's −7pp penalty entirely. The evolutionary search finds a more precise prior knowledge that makes case studies unnecessary.

6. **Best-of-N CS selection improves case study quality.** Pooling CS candidates under a loosened threshold and selecting by fix rate achieves +6pp over unlimited CS on GS and +3.7pp on snarks (best-of-1). The mechanism validates that CS quality varies substantially within a single task's Phase 2 run.

7. **PK size constraints can function as a regularizer.** Restricting Phase 1 output to 12K characters improves train accuracy over unlimited on two of four ablation tasks, suggesting that concision pressure at generation time reduces low-quality padding in the prior knowledge.
