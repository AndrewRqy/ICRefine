**Pronoun Antecedent Resolution Cheat Sheet for Difficult Cases**

---

### 1. **Ambiguous Pronouns Between Two People (Same Gender or Neutral)**
- When a sentence involves two people (e.g., "scientist and artist," "developer and writer") and a pronoun refers to one of them without clear contextual clues, **the antecedent is usually ambiguous**.
- Example structures:
  - "X collaborated with Y, and he/she/they did something."
  - "X argued with Y because he/she liked the design."
  - "X met with Y because she made a mistake."
- **Reasoning:** Both subjects can logically perform the action or possess the attribute. Without extra context, no clear choice.
- **Answer:** (C) Ambiguous

---

### 2. **Pronouns Referring to Possessive Forms with Multiple Entities**
- When a possessive pronoun (his/her/their) modifies a plural noun phrase involving two people (e.g., "the writer and her friends"), check:
  - Does the possessive pronoun logically refer to one of the named individuals?
  - Is it plausible for the other possible referents to own the noun?
- Example:
  - "The writer and her friends' support" → "her" most naturally refers to the writer, not the friends or an unrelated entity.
- **Answer:** The pronoun refers to the named individual (e.g., the writer).

---

### 3. **Pronouns Referring to Roles or Attributes Strongly Associated with One Person**
- Use real-world knowledge about roles or attributes to resolve pronouns:
  - "culinary training" → likely refers to the chef, not the customer.
  - "focuses on code" → likely refers to the developer, not the writer.
  - "upholds the peace" → likely refers to the sheriff, not the writer.
  - "grading policy" → likely refers to the educator, not the student.
- **Answer:** Pronoun refers to the person whose role or attribute fits the context.

---

### 4. **Pronouns Referring to the Object of an Action (Direct or Indirect Object)**
- When a sentence involves one person acting on another and then "asked/told him/her/them," the pronoun usually refers to the person being acted upon (the indirect object).
- Example:
  - "The guard called the cleaner and asked them to open the door." → "them" = cleaner (the one being asked).
  - "The designer showed the cook a blueprint and asked him for feedback." → "him" = cook.
  - "The writer tried to fool the salesperson and told him a fake story." → "him" = salesperson.
- **Answer:** Pronoun refers to the person receiving the action/request.

---

### 5. **Pronouns Referring to the Subject When Explaining Reason or Cause**
- When a sentence explains why an action happened "because he/she/they [did something]," check if the cause logically fits the subject or the object:
  - "The developer was unable to communicate with the writer because he focuses on code." → "he" = developer (fits role).
  - "The secretary corrected the developer because they better understood the problem." → "they" = secretary (the one correcting should understand better).
- If both interpretations are plausible, answer is ambiguous.

---

### 6. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Who Would Logically Perform an Action**
- When the pronoun is the subject of a clause describing an action or state, consider who would logically perform that action:
  - "The janitor warned the child not to step on the wet floor, otherwise she would have to mop it all over again." → "she" = janitor (responsible for mopping).
  - "The worker told the pedestrian that they were repairing the sidewalk." → "they" = worker (the one doing the repair).
- **Answer:** Pronoun refers to the person logically responsible for the action.

---

### 7. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Being Informed or Told Something**
- When someone "told" another person that "she/he did something," the pronoun usually refers to the speaker (subject), not the listener.
- Example:
  - "The technician told the customer that she had completed the repair." → "she" = technician.
  - "The hygienist told the patient that she should floss every day." → "she" = patient (advice given to patient).
- **Note:** If the pronoun is the object of advice or information, it often refers to the listener.

---

### 8. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Who Owns or Uses an Object**
- When a pronoun modifies a noun indicating possession or use (e.g., "her microscope," "his car"), it usually refers to the person who logically owns or uses the object.
- Example:
  - "The pathologist obtained tissue samples from the victim to look at under her microscope." → "her" = pathologist (victim unlikely to have microscope).
  - "The mechanic called to inform the customer that his car would be ready." → "his" = customer.
- **Answer:** Pronoun refers to the person logically associated with the object.

---

### 9. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Who Needs or Has a Reason**
- When a sentence explains an action "because he/she needed to..." or "because he/she is an expert," the pronoun usually refers to the person who logically has that need or expertise.
- Example:
  - "The undergraduate applied to work with the scientist because he needed a lab assistant." → "he" = scientist.
  - "The patient was referred to the specialist because he had a rare skin condition." → "he" = patient.
  - "The patient was referred to the specialist because he is an expert on rare skin conditions." → "he" = specialist.
- **Answer:** Pronoun refers to the person logically fitting the need or expertise.

---

### 10. **Pronouns in Sentences with Plural Subjects and Singular Pronouns**
- When a sentence has two subjects joined by "and" and a singular pronoun (his/her), the pronoun usually refers to one of the two individuals, not both.
- If the context strongly favors one individual (e.g., culinary training for chef), choose that individual.
- If no context favors one, answer is ambiguous.

---

### 11. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Who Is the Logical Recipient of an Action**
- When a person "gave him/her/them" something, the pronoun usually refers to the recipient, not the giver.
- Example:
  - "The carpenter collaborated with the designer and gave him a prototype." → "him" = designer.
  - "The carpenter made a desk for the clerk and gave them a discount." → "them" = clerk.
- **Answer:** Pronoun refers to the recipient.

---

### 12. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Who Is the Logical Subject of a Verb**
- When the pronoun is the subject of a verb that logically fits only one person, assign accordingly.
- Example:
  - "The mechanic greets the receptionist because he is standing in front of the door." → ambiguous (both possible).
  - "The mechanic greets the receptionist because she is standing in front of the door." → ambiguous (both possible).
- If both possible antecedents fit, answer is ambiguous.

---

### 13. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Who Is the Logical Object of a Verb**
- When the pronoun is the object of a verb like "warned," "told," or "asked," it usually refers to the person being warned, told, or asked.
- Example:
  - "The worker told the pedestrian that she should avoid the closed portion." → "she" = pedestrian.
- **Answer:** Pronoun refers to the person receiving the communication or action.

---

### 14. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Who Is the Logical Subject of a Clause Explaining a Reason**
- When a clause explains a reason for an action, the pronoun usually refers to the person who logically caused or justified the action.
- Example:
  - "The developer corrected the secretary because she better understood the problem." → "she" = developer (the corrector).
- If both possible antecedents fit, answer is ambiguous.

---

### 15. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Who Is the Logical Subject of a Clause Explaining a State or Condition**
- When a clause explains a state or condition (e.g., "because he smelled awful"), the pronoun usually refers to the person who logically has that state.
- Example:
  - "The nurse was running from the laborer because he smelled awful." → ambiguous (could be either).
- If both fit, answer is ambiguous.

---

### 16. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Who Is the Logical Subject of a Clause Explaining a Possession or Attribute**
- When a clause describes possession or attribute (e.g., "his favorite dishes"), and two people are involved, if no context favors one, answer is ambiguous.
- Example:
  - "The reporter and the chef discussed his favorite dishes." → ambiguous.

---

### 17. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Who Is the Logical Subject of a Clause Explaining a Past Action**
- When a clause describes a past action (e.g., "he had been working on the house"), and two people are involved, the pronoun usually refers to the person who logically performed the action.
- Example:
  - "The carpenter told the onlooker that he had been working on the house." → "he" = carpenter.
- If both fit, answer is ambiguous.

---

### 18. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Who Is the Logical Subject of a Clause Explaining a Statement or Disclosure**
- When a clause describes a disclosure or statement about a personal fact, the pronoun usually refers to the person making the disclosure.
- Example:
  - "The patient disclosed to the counselor that he had a history of substance abuse." → "he" = patient.

---

### 19. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Who Is the Logical Subject of a Clause Explaining a Need or Purpose**
- When a clause explains a need or purpose (e.g., "because he needed to understand the case"), the pronoun usually refers to the person who logically has that need.
- Example:
  - "The lawyer looked into accusations because he needed to understand the case." → "he" = lawyer.

---

### 20. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Who Is the Logical Subject of a Clause Explaining a Reason for an Action**
- When a clause explains a reason for an action (e.g., "because he is arrogant"), and both people could fit, answer is ambiguous.
- Example:
  - "The mechanic disliked the cashier because he is arrogant." → ambiguous.

---

### 21. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Who Is the Logical Subject of a Clause Explaining a Reason for an Action (Plural Pronouns)**
- When plural pronouns ("they," "them") are used and two singular people are involved, the pronoun usually refers to the person whose role fits the action.
- Example:
  - "The developer was unable to communicate with the writer because they used big words." → ambiguous (both plausible).

---

### 22. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Who Is the Logical Subject of a Clause Explaining a Reason for an Action (Singular Pronouns)**
- When singular pronouns ("he," "she") are used and two singular people are involved, use role knowledge to decide.
- Example:
  - "The developer was unable to communicate with the writer because he focuses on code." → "he" = developer.

---

### 23. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Who Is the Logical Subject of a Clause Explaining a Reason for an Action (Gender-Neutral or Plural Pronouns)**
- When gender-neutral or plural pronouns are used, and the sentence involves two people, if no context favors one, answer is ambiguous.

---

### 24. **Pronouns Referring to the Person Who Is the Logical Subject of a Clause Explaining a Reason for an Action (Pronouns with Number Mismatch)**
- When pronouns do not agree in number with possible antecedents, eliminate mismatches.
- Example:
  - "Alex tells us that he could not meet." → "he" cannot refer to "us" (plural), so "he" = Alex.

---

### Summary Table for Quick Reference

| Situation                                         | Strategy/Clue                                   | Answer Type          |
|-------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|----------------------|
| Two people, pronoun could refer to either       | Both plausible, no context                      | Ambiguous (C)        |
| Possessive pronoun with plural noun phrase      | Pronoun refers to named individual              | Named individual     |
| Role/attribute strongly associated with one     | Use real-world knowledge                         | That person          |
| Pronoun as indirect object of action            | Pronoun refers to person acted upon              | That person          |
| Reason/cause clause                              | Pronoun refers to person logically causing reason| That person or ambiguous |
| Logical owner/user of object                     | Pronoun refers to logical owner/user             | That person          |
| Person being informed/told                       | Pronoun refers to listener or subject depending on context | Usually subject or listener |
| Plural subjects with singular pronoun           | Ambiguous unless context clarifies               | Ambiguous or context |
| Number mismatch                                  | Eliminate mismatches                              | Matching antecedent  |

---

**Use this cheat sheet to analyze pronoun antecedents by:**

1. Identifying all possible antecedents.
2. Considering sentence structure and verb roles.
3. Applying real-world knowledge about roles and attributes.
4. Checking for number and gender agreement.
5. Determining if context clarifies or if ambiguity remains.
6. Selecting the most logical antecedent or marking ambiguous if unclear.
