**SVG Path Shapes Cheat Sheet for Difficult Examples**

---

### 1. **Understanding SVG Path Commands**

- **M x,y**: Move to coordinate (x,y) without drawing.
- **L x,y**: Draw a straight line from current point to (x,y).
- **A rx,ry x-axis-rotation large-arc-flag,sweep-flag x,y**: Draw an elliptical arc from current point to (x,y) with radii rx, ry, rotation, and flags.

---

### 2. **Identifying Polygons by Number of Sides**

- Count **unique points connected by lines** (ignore repeated moves without drawing).
- Closed shape if path returns to starting point.
- Polygon names by sides:
  - 3 sides → **Triangle**
  - 4 sides → **Quadrilateral** (see special cases below)
  - 5 sides → **Pentagon**
  - 6 sides → **Hexagon**
  - 7 sides → **Heptagon**
  - 8 sides → **Octagon**

---

### 3. **Distinguishing Quadrilaterals**

When the shape has 4 sides, options usually include:

- **Rectangle**:  
  - Opposite sides equal and parallel.  
  - All angles 90°.  
  - Coordinates show vertical/horizontal lines with equal lengths.

- **Kite**:  
  - Two pairs of adjacent sides equal in length.  
  - Not necessarily parallel sides.  
  - Check side lengths: adjacent pairs equal, opposite sides usually unequal.

- **Trapezoid**:  
  - At least one pair of parallel sides.  
  - Other sides not necessarily equal or parallel.  
  - Coordinates often show two sides with same y or x values (horizontal or vertical), but not all sides parallel.

**How to decide:**

- Calculate distances between consecutive points to check side lengths.
- Check if pairs of sides are parallel by comparing coordinates:
  - Horizontal sides: y-coordinates equal.
  - Vertical sides: x-coordinates equal.
- Check angles if possible (right angles for rectangle).
- If two pairs of adjacent sides equal → kite.
- If only one pair of parallel sides → trapezoid.
- If two pairs of parallel sides and right angles → rectangle.

---

### 4. **Identifying Curved Shapes (Arcs)**

- Presence of **"A" commands** indicates arcs (elliptical or circular).
- Two consecutive arcs with same radii and rotation, returning to start → **Ellipse**.
- If radii equal and no rotation → **Circle** (special case of ellipse).
- Arc combined with two straight lines forming a wedge → **Sector** (pie slice).
- Arc with two straight lines returning to start → sector shape.

---

### 5. **Handling Multiple Subpaths (Multiple "M" commands)**

- Multiple "M" commands split the path into subpaths.
- To identify shape:
  - Consider all connected points that form a closed loop.
  - Ignore moves without drawing.
  - Count unique points connected by lines in the closed loop.
- Sometimes subpaths connect to form a polygon with multiple sides.

---

### 6. **Summary of Difficult Cases**

| Shape Type | Key Identifiers | Notes |
|------------|-----------------|-------|
| **Heptagon (7 sides)** | 7 unique connected points forming closed path | Count carefully, include all connected points ignoring moves without drawing |
| **Hexagon (6 sides)** | 6 unique connected points forming closed path | Same as above |
| **Octagon (8 sides)** | 8 unique connected points forming closed path | Same as above |
| **Pentagon (5 sides)** | 5 unique connected points forming closed path | Same as above |
| **Trapezoid** | 4 sides, exactly one pair of parallel sides | Check coordinate equality for parallelism; side lengths may vary |
| **Kite** | 4 sides, two pairs of adjacent equal sides | Calculate side lengths; adjacent pairs equal |
| **Rectangle** | 4 sides, two pairs of parallel sides, all angles 90° | Coordinates show vertical/horizontal lines; equal opposite sides |
| **Sector** | Path with arcs and two straight lines forming wedge | Look for "A" commands with two lines closing the shape |
| **Ellipse** | Two arcs with same radii and rotation forming closed curve | "A" commands with equal rx, ry and rotation |
| **Line** | Only two points connected by one line segment | Only one "M" and one "L" command, no closure |

---

### 7. **Tips for Quick Identification**

- **Count points carefully**: Include all points connected by lines, ignore moves without drawing.
- **Check closure**: Does the path return to the starting point?
- **Look for arcs**: Presence of "A" commands means curved shapes (ellipse, sector).
- **For quadrilaterals**:  
  - Check side lengths and parallelism to distinguish kite, trapezoid, rectangle.
- **Multiple subpaths**: Combine connected points to find polygon sides.
- **If only two points and one line**: It's a line.

---

### 8. **Example Quick Checks**

- Path with 7 unique points connected → **Heptagon**.
- Path with 6 unique points connected → **Hexagon**.
- Path with 8 unique points connected → **Octagon**.
- Path with 4 points, two pairs of adjacent equal sides → **Kite**.
- Path with 4 points, one pair of parallel sides → **Trapezoid**.
- Path with arcs forming closed curve → **Ellipse**.
- Path with arcs and two straight lines forming wedge → **Sector**.
- Path with only two points and one line → **Line**.
