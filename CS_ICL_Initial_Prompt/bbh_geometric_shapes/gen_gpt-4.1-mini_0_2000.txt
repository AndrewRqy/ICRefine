**SVG Path Identification Cheat Sheet for Difficult Cases**

---

### 1. **Understanding SVG Commands**

- **M x,y**: Move to coordinate (x,y) without drawing.
- **L x,y**: Draw a straight line from the current point to (x,y).
- **A rx,ry x-axis-rotation large-arc-flag,sweep-flag x,y**: Draw an elliptical arc from the current point to (x,y) with radii rx, ry, rotation, and flags.

---

### 2. **Identifying Polygon Types**

- **Count Unique Points Connected by Lines**  
  - The number of unique points connected in a closed loop = number of polygon sides.
  - Examples:  
    - 3 points → Triangle  
    - 4 points → Quadrilateral (kite, rectangle, trapezoid)  
    - 5 points → Pentagon  
    - 6 points → Hexagon  
    - 7 points → Heptagon  
    - 8 points → Octagon

- **Closed Shape**: The last point connects back to the first point.

---

### 3. **Distinguishing Quadrilaterals**

When the shape has **4 sides**, distinguish between:

- **Kite**  
  - Two pairs of adjacent sides are equal in length.  
  - Adjacent sides equal, opposite sides not necessarily equal.  
  - Check side lengths:  
    - If sides A & B adjacent equal, and sides C & D adjacent equal → Kite.

- **Rectangle**  
  - Opposite sides equal and all angles 90°.  
  - Sides parallel to axes (horizontal/vertical) often indicate rectangle.  
  - Check coordinates for equal x or y values for opposite sides.

- **Trapezoid**  
  - At least one pair of parallel sides.  
  - Usually two sides parallel (check if y or x coordinates are equal or nearly equal for a pair).  
  - Side lengths may differ; angles not necessarily 90°.  
  - Rectangle is a special case of trapezoid, but if both present, prefer rectangle if all angles are right.

---

### 4. **Identifying Ellipses and Circles**

- **Use of "A" (arc) commands with two arcs connecting start and end points**  
  - Two "A" commands with same rx and ry, connecting start → mid → start, form a closed ellipse or circle.

- **Circle vs Ellipse**  
  - If rx = ry and rotation = 0°, it's a circle.  
  - If rx ≠ ry or rotation ≠ 0°, it's an ellipse.  
  - If rotation is nonzero but rx = ry, still considered ellipse (rotated circle).  
  - When in doubt, answer "ellipse" if rotation or arc flags are nonzero.

---

### 5. **Identifying Sector (Pie Slice)**

- **Combination of "M", "L", and "A" commands** forming:  
  - Move to center point (M)  
  - Line to arc start point (L)  
  - Arc (A) to arc end point  
  - Line back to center (L)  
- This forms a sector (a slice of a circle or ellipse).

---

### 6. **Identifying Lines**

- Only two points connected by a single "L" command after "M".  
- No closed shape or multiple points.

---

### 7. **Step-by-Step Approach for Difficult Cases**

1. **List all unique points connected by lines (ignore moves without lines).**

2. **Count the number of unique points connected in a closed loop.**

3. **If shape is closed:**

   - If 3 points → Triangle  
   - If 4 points → Check side lengths and parallelism for kite, rectangle, trapezoid  
   - If 5+ points → Polygon with corresponding name (pentagon, hexagon, heptagon, octagon)

4. **If arcs ("A") are present:**

   - Two arcs forming a closed loop with equal radii → Ellipse (or circle if no rotation)  
   - Arc plus two lines forming a wedge → Sector

5. **If only two points connected → Line**

---

### 8. **Calculating Side Lengths (for Quadrilaterals)**

Use distance formula between points (x1,y1) and (x2,y2):

\[
\text{distance} = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}
\]

- Compare adjacent sides for equality (kite).  
- Compare opposite sides and angles for rectangle.  
- Check for parallel sides by comparing y or x coordinates (trapezoid).

---

### 9. **Summary Table**

| Shape     | Key Features in SVG Path                                  | Identification Tips                          |
|-----------|-----------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| **Line**  | 2 points, 1 line segment                                   | Only M and L with 2 points                    |
| **Triangle** | 3 unique points connected in closed loop                 | 3 points, closed path                         |
| **Kite**  | 4 points, 2 pairs of adjacent equal sides                 | Check side lengths, adjacent pairs equal     |
| **Rectangle** | 4 points, opposite sides equal, right angles             | Parallel sides with equal length, right angles|
| **Trapezoid** | 4 points, at least one pair of parallel sides            | Check for parallel sides, not rectangle or kite|
| **Pentagon** | 5 unique points connected in closed loop                  | 5 points, closed path                         |
| **Hexagon** | 6 unique points connected in closed loop                  | 6 points, closed path                         |
| **Heptagon** | 7 unique points connected in closed loop                  | 7 points, closed path                         |
| **Octagon** | 8 unique points connected in closed loop                  | 8 points, closed path                         |
| **Ellipse** | 2 "A" commands with equal rx, ry, forming closed loop     | Elliptical arcs with rotation or flags       |
| **Circle** | Ellipse with rx=ry and rotation=0                         | Special case of ellipse                        |
| **Sector** | Combination of M, L, A commands forming wedge shape        | Arc plus two lines to center                   |

---

### 10. **Tips for Quick Recognition**

- **Multiple "M" commands with immediate "L" to same point**: Path is split into subpaths; consider only connected points for polygon count.

- **If path uses only M and L with 2 points**: It's a line.

- **If path uses M, L, and two A commands forming a closed loop**: Ellipse or circle.

- **If path uses M, L, and A commands forming a wedge (two lines + arc)**: Sector.

- **For quadrilaterals, always check side lengths and parallelism to distinguish kite, rectangle, trapezoid.**
