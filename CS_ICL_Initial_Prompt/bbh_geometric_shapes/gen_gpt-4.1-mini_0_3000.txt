**SVG Path Shapes Cheat Sheet for Difficult Examples**

---

### 1. **Identifying Polygons by Number of Sides**

- **Count unique vertices connected in a closed loop** (ignore repeated points).
- **Polygon names by number of sides:**
  - 3 sides → **Triangle**
  - 4 sides → Could be **Kite**, **Rectangle**, or **Trapezoid** (see below)
  - 5 sides → **Pentagon**
  - 6 sides → **Hexagon**
  - 7 sides → **Heptagon**
  - 8 sides → **Octagon**

---

### 2. **Distinguishing Quadrilaterals (4-sided polygons)**

- **Kite:**
  - Two pairs of adjacent sides are equal in length.
  - Adjacent sides equal, opposite sides not necessarily equal.
  - Angles are not necessarily 90°.
  - Check side lengths by distance formula:
    \[
    d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}
    \]
  - If two pairs of adjacent sides are equal → likely a kite.

- **Rectangle:**
  - Opposite sides equal and parallel.
  - All angles are 90°.
  - Sides lengths: opposite sides equal.
  - Coordinates often show vertical and horizontal lines (same x or y values).
  - If all angles 90° and opposite sides equal → rectangle.

- **Trapezoid:**
  - At least one pair of parallel sides.
  - Other sides not parallel.
  - Side lengths may vary.
  - Check slopes of sides:
    \[
    m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}
    \]
  - If exactly one pair of sides have equal slopes → trapezoid.
  - Rectangle is a special case of trapezoid (two pairs parallel).

---

### 3. **Recognizing Lines**

- Only two points connected by a single "M" and "L" command.
- No closed shape.
- Answer: **Line**

---

### 4. **Recognizing Ellipses and Circles**

- SVG path uses **"A" (arc)** commands with equal radii (rx = ry).
- Two "A" commands forming a closed loop → ellipse or circle.
- If rotation ≠ 0 → ellipse.
- If rotation = 0 and rx = ry → circle (but often answer is ellipse).
- Ellipse is the safest answer for arcs with rotation or non-zero flags.
- Example:
  ```
  M x,y A rx,ry rotation large-arc-flag,sweep-flag x,y A rx,ry rotation large-arc-flag,sweep-flag x,y
  ```
- Closed path with two arcs → ellipse.

---

### 5. **Recognizing Sectors**

- Path contains:
  - Move to center point.
  - Line to arc start point.
  - Arc command ("A") for part of circle or ellipse.
  - Line back to center.
- Shape looks like a "pie slice".
- Two straight lines (radii) + one arc.
- Answer: **Sector**

---

### 6. **General Approach to Difficult Shapes**

- **Step 1:** Identify commands:
  - "M" = move to (start new subpath)
  - "L" = line to
  - "A" = arc to (elliptical arc)
- **Step 2:** Count unique points connected by lines in a closed loop.
- **Step 3:** For polygons:
  - Count sides.
  - Check if closed (last point connects to first).
- **Step 4:** For quadrilaterals:
  - Calculate side lengths.
  - Calculate slopes to check parallelism.
  - Determine if kite, rectangle, or trapezoid.
- **Step 5:** For arcs:
  - If two arcs with equal radii and closed → ellipse.
  - If combined with lines from center → sector.
- **Step 6:** For single line segments → line.

---

### 7. **Formulas**

- **Distance between points (x1,y1) and (x2,y2):**
  \[
  d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}
  \]

- **Slope between points (x1,y1) and (x2,y2):**
  \[
  m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}
  \]
  - Parallel lines have equal slopes.
  - Vertical lines have undefined slope (division by zero).

---

### 8. **Tips**

- Multiple "M" commands often indicate subpaths; focus on connected points forming closed loops.
- When in doubt about quadrilaterals, check side lengths and slopes.
- For arcs, if the path closes with two arcs of equal radii, pick ellipse.
- If arcs plus two lines from a point → sector.
- Count vertices carefully; do not count repeated points twice.
- For polygons, the number of unique vertices = number of sides.
