**Causation Cheat Sheet for Difficult Examples**

---

### 1. **Causation in Cases of Multiple Sufficient Causes (Overdetermination)**

- **Key idea:** If an outcome would have happened due to one cause alone, the other cause(s) are *not* seen as causing the outcome.

- **Example:**  
  - *Alex wins if dice > 11 OR spinner lands green.*  
  - Both happen, but dice > 11 alone suffices.  
  - **Answer:** Spinner landing green did **not** cause the win.

- **How to apply:**  
  - Identify if one cause alone would have produced the outcome.  
  - If yes, other simultaneous causes are not considered causes of the outcome.

---

### 2. **Causation in Cases of Joint or Combined Causes (Conjunctive Conditions)**

- **Key idea:** When an outcome requires *both* conditions to be met (AND), each condition is a cause if it was necessary for the outcome.

- **Example:**  
  - *Building implodes if safety switch OFF AND knob A ON.*  
  - Safety switch is OFF, knob A is OFF. Tom turns knob A ON → building implodes.  
  - **Answer:** Tom’s action caused the implosion.

- **How to apply:**  
  - Check if both conditions are necessary.  
  - If one condition was missing and the outcome would not occur, the action that fulfilled that condition is a cause.

---

### 3. **Causation in Cases of Neglect or Omission**

- **Key idea:** Failure to act can be a cause if:  
  - The person had responsibility or duty to act, and  
  - Their omission directly led to the outcome.

- **Example:**  
  - *Janet responsible for oiling machine; she forgets; machine breaks down.*  
  - Kate knows but is not responsible and does not oil.  
  - **Answer:** Janet’s omission caused breakdown; Kate’s omission does **not** cause breakdown if no responsibility.

- **When responsibility shifts:**  
  - If primary responsible person fails, and secondary responsible person *has* duty to act, their omission can also be a cause.

---

### 4. **Causation and Knowledge/Intent**

- **Intentional causation:**  
  - Person is aware that their action will cause an outcome and proceeds anyway.  
  - Even if they do not care about the outcome, foreknowledge + action = intentional causation.

- **Unintentional causation:**  
  - Outcome is a side effect or accident, not the person’s goal or intention.  
  - Loss of control (e.g., hand slips) means no intentional causation.

- **Examples:**  
  - *Lieutenant orders soldiers into line of fire knowing some will die → intentional.*  
  - *Hunter shoots deer knowing eagle will fly away but does not care → not intentional for eagle flying.*  
  - *Jake’s hand slips but bullet hits heart → not intentional.*

---

### 5. **Causation in Cases of Rule Violation and Norms**

- **If a person violates a rule knowingly and their action contributes to the outcome, they are seen as causing the problem.**

- **If a person is unaware of the rule and acts accordingly, typical people tend to *not* blame them for causing the problem.**

- **Example:**  
  - *Hedgehog absent when pencil rule announced, takes pencils → causes problem.*  
  - *Hedgehog present and knows rule, still takes pencils → less likely to be seen as cause.*

---

### 6. **Causation in Cases of Policy Compliance vs. Violation**

- **If one person follows policy and another violates it, and the outcome depends on both, typical people blame the violator.**

- **Example:**  
  - *Suzy logs in at permitted time; Billy logs in when not permitted; emails deleted.*  
  - **Answer:** Billy caused deletion, Suzy did not.

---

### 7. **Causation in Cases of Multiple Contributors with Different Roles**

- **If harm results from a combination of actions, each necessary contributor is a cause.**

- **If one contributor followed instructions and the other did not, both may be causes if their combined actions caused harm.**

- **Example:**  
  - *Alex uses chemical A; Benni uses chemical B; plants dry out only where both applied.*  
  - Alex forgot to tell Benni → both caused harm.  
  - If Alex followed instructions and Benni knowingly used wrong chemical → Benni caused harm, Alex did not alone.

---

### 8. **Causation in Cases of Foreseeable but Unintended Side Effects**

- **Foreseeing a side effect but not intending it does not count as intentional causation.**

- **Example:**  
  - *Bob robs bank, knows neurotoxin will improve town health but does not care → not intentional improvement.*

---

### 9. **Causation in Cases of Multiple People Contributing to a Threshold**

- **If an outcome occurs only when a threshold number of people act, each person whose action was necessary to reach that threshold is a cause.**

- **Example:**  
  - *Account suspended if 3 people log in simultaneously.*  
  - Todd, Anne, Jason log in together → Todd caused suspension because without him only 2 would be logged in.

- **If the person’s action was usual and the outcome depends on others joining, typical people may not blame the usual actor.**

---

### 10. **Causation in Cases of Intent vs. Mistake**

- **If a person acts with the goal to cause an outcome, even if mistaken about details, they are seen as intentional.**

- **Example:**  
  - *Mark wants to detonate bomb, presses wrong button but it detonates → intentional.*

---

### 11. **Causation in Cases of Foreseeable Harm but Indifference**

- **Knowing harm will occur and proceeding anyway counts as intentional causation, even if the person is indifferent.**

- **Example:**  
  - *Man pumps poisoned water knowing it will kill → intentional poisoning.*

---

### 12. **Causation in Cases of Outcome Already Guaranteed**

- **If an outcome would have happened regardless of a particular action, that action is not seen as causing the outcome.**

- **Example:**  
  - *Laurie already qualified for free sample by previous purchase; subscribing to email list not necessary → not cause.*

---

### 13. **Causation in Cases of Accidental Success**

- **If success occurs despite loss of control or accident, typical people do not see it as intentional.**

- **Example:**  
  - *Jake’s hand slips but hits bull’s-eye → not intentional.*

---

### 14. **Causation in Cases of Foreseeable but Unintended Collateral Damage**

- **If a person foresees collateral damage but does not intend it, typical people say the collateral damage was not intentional.**

- **Exception:** If the person acts with disregard and foreknowledge, some may say intentional (see bird-watcher example).

---

### 15. **Causation in Cases of Responsibility Transfer**

- **If primary responsible person fails, and secondary responsible person has duty to act, failure of secondary person can be cause.**

- **If secondary person has no responsibility, their omission is not cause.**

---

---

**Use this cheat sheet to analyze each question step-by-step:**

1. Identify the **type of causation scenario** (multiple causes, omission, intent, etc.).  
2. Determine if the action was **necessary and/or sufficient** for the outcome.  
3. Check if the person had **responsibility or duty** to act.  
4. Assess if the person had **knowledge and intention** regarding the outcome.  
5. Consider if the outcome would have happened **regardless** of the action.  
6. Apply the typical person’s reasoning based on the above principles.

---

**Keep these principles in mind to answer causation questions accurately without referring back to examples.**
