## DATE CALCULATION CHEAT SHEET

### 1. **Adding/Subtracting Days Across Months**
- **To add/subtract days:**  
  - If the result goes past the end of the month, move to the next month and subtract remaining days.
  - If subtracting and you go before the 1st, move to the previous month and add the number of days in that month.
- **Days in each month:**
  - Jan: 31, Feb: 28 (29 if leap year), Mar: 31, Apr: 30, May: 31, Jun: 30, Jul: 31, Aug: 31, Sep: 30, Oct: 31, Nov: 30, Dec: 31

#### **Example:**
- May 9 + 40 days:
  - May 9–May 31 = 23 days (May 9 counts as day 1)
  - 40 - 23 = 17 days into June → June 17 is day 39, June 18 is day 40

### 2. **Leap Year Rules**
- Leap year if:
  - Year divisible by 4 **and** (not divisible by 100 **or** divisible by 400)
- **Feb 29 exists only in leap years.**
- If adding/subtracting a year from Feb 29 and the result year is not a leap year, use Feb 28.

#### **Examples:**
- 2000 is leap (Feb 29 exists), 2001 is not (Feb 28 is last day)
- 2016 is leap, 2017 is not

### 3. **UK vs US Date Formats**
- **UK:** DD/MM/YYYY (day first)
- **US:** MM/DD/YYYY (month first)
- When converting, swap day and month.

#### **Example:**
- UK: 02/01/1987 = Jan 2, 1987 (US: 01/02/1987)

### 4. **Special Dates**
- **Last day of month:**  
  - Jan: 31, Feb: 28/29, Mar: 31, Apr: 30, May: 31, Jun: 30, Jul: 31, Aug: 31, Sep: 30, Oct: 31, Nov: 30, Dec: 31
- **First day of year:** 01/01/YYYY
- **Christmas Eve:** 12/24/YYYY
- **Christmas Day:** 12/25/YYYY
- **Thanksgiving (US):** 4th Thursday in November

### 5. **Anniversary Calculations**
- **N-year anniversary:** Add N years to the original date.
- If original date is Feb 29 and anniversary year is not leap, use Feb 28.

### 6. **"Nth day after/before"**
- **"24 hours later"** = next day
- **"One week from today"** = +7 days
- **"One week ago"** = -7 days
- **"A month ago"** = same day, previous month (if that day exists; otherwise, last day of previous month)
- **"A year ago"** = same month/day, previous year (adjust for leap years)

### 7. **Counting Days Example**
- **To find date N days after a given date:**
  1. Count remaining days in current month (including start day).
  2. If N > days left, subtract days left, move to next month, repeat.
  3. If N < days left, just add N to the day.

#### **Example:**
- Mar 20 + 176 days:
  - Mar: 11 days (Mar 20–Mar 31)
  - Apr: 30
  - May: 31
  - Jun: 30
  - Jul: 31
  - Aug: 31
  - Total after Aug: 11+30+31+30+31+31 = 164
  - 176 - 164 = 12 days into September → Sep 12

### 8. **"Nth Visit" or "Nth Occurrence"**
- If starting on a specific day each month, the Nth occurrence is N-1 months after the start.
  - E.g., 1st: Oct 16, 2nd: Nov 16, 3rd: Dec 16, 4th: Jan 16, 5th: Feb 16

### 9. **Holidays**
- **Thanksgiving (US):** 4th Thursday in November
  - Find the first Thursday, then add 21 days (3 weeks) to get the 4th Thursday.

## QUICK REFERENCE TABLE

| Operation                | How to Calculate                                                                 |
|--------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Add/Subtract 1 day       | Next/previous calendar day                                                       |
| Add/Subtract 7 days      | Next/previous week (same weekday)                                                |
| Add/Subtract 1 month     | Same day, previous/next month (if day exists, else last day of month)            |
| Add/Subtract 1 year      | Same month/day, previous/next year (adjust for leap years)                       |
| UK to US date            | Swap day and month                                                               |
| Last day of month        | See table above                                                                  |
| Leap year                | Divisible by 4, not by 100 unless also by 400                                    |
| Nth occurrence/month     | Start date + (N-1) months                                                        |
| Counting days across months | Subtract days in current month, move to next, repeat as needed                |

## TIPS

- **Always check if the month has enough days when adding/subtracting months.**
- **For "a month ago" from March 31, use Feb 28/29 if February has no 31st.**
- **For "one year ago" from Feb 29, use Feb 28 if not a leap year.**
- **For "Nth day after/before," count carefully across month boundaries.**
- **For UK dates, always check if the day is >12 to avoid confusion.**
- **For holidays, look up the rule (e.g., Thanksgiving = 4th Thursday in November).**

Question: Yesterday was 12/31/1929. Today could not be 12/32/1929 because December has only 31 days. What is the date today in MM/DD/YYYY?
Options:
(A) 01/08/1930
(B) 01/06/1930
(C) 01/01/1930
(D) 12/31/1929
(E) 10/01/1930
(F) 02/27/1930
Reasoning: Let's think step by step.  
We are told that "Yesterday was 12/31/1929." The day after December 31st is January 1st of the next year, since December only has 31 days. Therefore, today is 01/01/1930. In MM/DD/YYYY format this is 01/01/1930. So the answer is (C).
Answer: (C)

Question: In the UK, people usually put the day before the month when formatting the date. Therefore, today is 02/01/1987 to them. What is the date yesterday in MM/DD/YYYY?
Options:
(A) 01/01/1987
(B) 10/19/1986
(C) 01/08/1987
(D) 01/01/2015
(E) 01/01/1934
(F) 12/29/1986
Reasoning: Let's think step by step.  
In the UK, dates are written as day/month/year, so 02/01/1987 means January 2, 1987. Therefore, today is 01/02/1987 in MM/DD/YYYY format. Yesterday would be one day before that, which is January 1, 1987, or 01/01/1987 in MM/DD/YYYY format. So the answer is (A).
Answer: (A)
