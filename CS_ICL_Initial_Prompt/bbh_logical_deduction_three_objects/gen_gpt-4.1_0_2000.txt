## CHEAT SHEET: 3-Object Ordering & Ranking Problems

### 1. **General Approach**
- **List the objects:** Write down the three items (A, B, C).
- **Translate statements into orderings:** For each statement, write the relationship as an inequality or position (e.g., "A is to the left of B" → A < B).
- **Combine statements:** Use both statements to deduce the full order.
- **Answer the specific question:** Once the order is clear, answer the question about position or ranking.

---

### 2. **Translating Statements**

#### **A. Left/Right or Above/Below**
- "X is to the left of Y" → X < Y
- "X is to the right of Y" → X > Y
- "X finished above Y" (in rankings) → X < Y (X is better)
- "X finished below Y" → X > Y (X is worse)

#### **B. Most/Least Expensive, Oldest/Newest**
- "X is more expensive than Y" → X > Y
- "X is less expensive than Y" → X < Y
- "X is older than Y" → X > Y
- "X is newer than Y" → X < Y

#### **C. Position Statements**
- "X is the leftmost" → X is first
- "X is the rightmost" → X is last
- "X is the second from the left" → X is in the middle

---

### 3. **Combining Statements: Step-by-Step**

#### **A. Chain the Relations**
- If you have "A < B" and "B < C", then the order is A < B < C.
- If you have "A < B" and "C < A", then the order is C < A < B.

#### **B. Use Given Positions**
- If a statement says "X is the second from the left," place X in the middle, then use other clues to fill in the rest.

#### **C. Ranking/Price/Age**
- Arrange from least to greatest (or vice versa) based on the clues.
- For "cheapest" or "oldest," remember:
    - "Cheapest" = lowest price = leftmost in price order.
    - "Oldest" = highest age = leftmost in age order.

---

### 4. **Sample Workflows**

#### **A. Example: Books on a Shelf**
- "Purple is to the left of Red." → Purple < Red
- "Gray is to the left of Purple." → Gray < Purple
- Combine: Gray < Purple < Red

#### **B. Example: Golfers' Rankings**
- "Eve finished below Amy." → Eve > Amy
- "Eve finished above Joe." → Eve < Joe
- Combine: Joe < Eve < Amy (Joe best, Amy worst)

#### **C. Example: Fruit Prices**
- "Loquats are the cheapest." → Loquats < others
- "Plums are less expensive than apples." → Plums < Apples
- Combine: Loquats < Plums < Apples

---

### 5. **Special Cases**

#### **A. "Second from the left"/"Second-most expensive"**
- Place the specified object in the middle.
- Use other clues to determine the order of the remaining two.

#### **B. "Oldest"/"Newest"**
- "X is older than Y" → X > Y (in age)
- "X is newer than Y" → X < Y (in age)
- Arrange accordingly.

### 7. **Tips**
- **Write out the order explicitly** (e.g., A < B < C).
- **Double-check the direction** (left/right, above/below, more/less).
- **If stuck, try all possible orders** and see which fits all clues.
- **For "second-most expensive" or "second-newest,"** remember it's the middle item in the order.

Question: The following paragraphs each describe a set of three objects arranged in a fixed order. The statements are logically consistent within each paragraph. On a shelf, there are three books: a gray book, a purple book, and a red book. The purple book is to the left of the red book. The gray book is to the left of the purple book.
Options:
(A) The gray book is the leftmost
(B) The purple book is the leftmost
(C) The red book is the leftmost
Reasoning: Let's think step by step. (1) The white book is to the left of the gray book: "(left) ? white ? gray ? (right)". (2) The red book is the second from the left: "(left) ? white red gray ? (right)". (3) Combining (1) and (2) we get the following ordering: "(left) white red gray (right)". According to this ordering, the leftmost book is the white book. The white book is the leftmost. So the answer is (C).
Answer: (A)
