Certainly! Here’s a **cheat sheet** focused on the most challenging aspects of these ordering/logic problems, especially those involving:

- **Relative ordering with "older/newer", "more/less expensive", "above/below", "left/right"**
- **Identifying "second from the left/right", "second-most expensive", "second-newest", etc.**
- **Translating statements into a concrete order**

---

## 1. **Translating Statements into Order**

### A. **Left/Right Positioning**
- "A is to the left of B" → A ... B
- "A is to the right of B" → B ... A

#### Example:
- "Quail is to the left of Owl. Owl is to the left of Hummingbird."
  - Quail ... Owl ... Hummingbird

### B. **Above/Below (Rankings/Finishing Order)**
- "A finished above B" → A is better (higher rank) than B
- "A finished below B" → A is worse (lower rank) than B

#### Example:
- "Rob finished below Mel. Mel finished below Eve."
  - Eve ... Mel ... Rob (Eve is best, Rob is worst)

### C. **Older/Newer (Age/Year)**
- "A is older than B" → A ... B (A is earlier)
- "A is newer than B" → B ... A (A is later)

#### Example:
- "Limousine is older than Bus. Bus is older than Convertible."
  - Limousine ... Bus ... Convertible (Limousine is oldest)

### D. **More/Less Expensive**
- "A is more expensive than B" → A ... B (A is higher price)
- "A is less expensive than B" → B ... A (A is lower price)

#### Example:
- "Pears are more expensive than Loquats. Pears are less expensive than Apples."
  - Apples ... Pears ... Loquats (Apples most expensive)

---

## 2. **Finding "Second from the Left/Right", "Second-most Expensive", etc.**

- Once you have the order (Left → Right, Most → Least, etc.), the **middle item** is the "second from the left", "second-most expensive", "second-newest", etc.

#### Example:
- Order: A, B, C
  - Leftmost/Most Expensive/Oldest: **A**
  - Second from left/Second-most expensive/Second-oldest: **B**
  - Rightmost/Least Expensive/Newest: **C**

---

## 3. **Step-by-Step Approach**

1. **Write down all items:** List the three objects.
2. **Apply each statement:** Use arrows or placeholders to show relationships.
3. **Combine statements:** Build the full order.
4. **Answer the question:** Identify which item matches the position asked (leftmost, rightmost, second, etc.).

---

## 4. **Special Phrases**

- **"Second from the left/right":** The middle item in the order.
- **"Second-most expensive":** The middle item in descending price order.
- **"Second-newest":** The middle item in ascending age order (newest to oldest).
- **"Second from the top/bottom":** The middle item in ranking.

---

## 5. **Common Pitfalls**

- **Don’t reverse the order:** "A is to the left of B" is NOT the same as "B is to the left of A".
- **Pay attention to "above/below":** "Above" means better/higher rank; "below" means worse/lower rank.
- **"Older" vs. "Newer":** "Older" means earlier, "newer" means later.
- **"More expensive" vs. "Less expensive":** "More expensive" is higher price, "less expensive" is lower price.

---

## 6. **Practice Example**

**Question:**  
Three cars: Sedan, Tractor, Bus.  
- Sedan is older than Tractor.  
- Bus is older than Sedan.

**Step-by-step:**
- Sedan ... Tractor (Sedan is older)
- Bus ... Sedan (Bus is older)
- Combine: Bus ... Sedan ... Tractor

**Oldest:** Bus  
**Second-newest:** Sedan  
**Newest:** Tractor

**Summary:**  
- Always translate statements into a linear order.
- The "second" item is always the middle one.
- Double-check the direction of each relationship.
- Use placeholders if needed: (? left ? middle ? right ?)

**If stuck:**  
- Draw the order as you go.
- Check each statement against your order.
- Confirm the question is asking for leftmost, rightmost, second, etc.

Question: The following paragraphs each describe a set of three objects arranged in a fixed order. The statements are logically consistent within each paragraph. On a branch, there are three birds: a quail, an owl, and a hummingbird. The quail is to the left of the owl. The owl is to the left of the hummingbird.
Options:
(A) The quail is the leftmost
(B) The owl is the leftmost
(C) The hummingbird is the leftmost
Reasoning: Let's think step by step. (1) The white book is to the left of the gray book: "(left) ? white ? gray ? (right)". (2) The red book is the second from the left: "(left) ? white red gray ? (right)". (3) Combining (1) and (2) we get the following ordering: "(left) white red gray (right)". According to this ordering, the leftmost book is the white book. The white book is the leftmost. So the answer is (C).
Answer: (A)
