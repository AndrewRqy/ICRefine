**Cheat Sheet for Identifying Sarcasm in Statements**

---

### Key Indicators of Sarcasm

1. **Contradiction Between Description and Conclusion**  
   - When the statement describes a negative or undesirable situation but concludes with a positive or praising phrase (or vice versa).  
   - Example pattern: Negative facts + positive praise = sarcasm.  
   - Example: Listing stressful events then calling it a "fun semester."

2. **Exaggeration or Absurdity**  
   - Statements that exaggerate to an unrealistic or absurd degree to mock or highlight a point.  
   - Example: Suggesting banning breathing, or that a $1000 phone should be dumpstered for bad battery life.  
   - Look for hyperbole that no one would sincerely endorse.

3. **Opposite of Common Sense or Reality**  
   - Statements that claim something obviously false or contrary to common knowledge as if it were true.  
   - Example: Claiming "no one is on the internet," or "armed revolutionaries have been making a lot of positive change lately."  
   - Sarcasm often uses this to highlight the absurdity of a situation.

4. **Praise for Negative Traits or Actions**  
   - When a statement praises or calls "brilliant," "wonderful," or "classy" something widely regarded as negative, harmful, or ridiculous.  
   - Example: Calling someone "brilliant" for polluting space with junk, or "classy" for mocking people trying to get healthier.

5. **Mocking Unrealistic or Oversimplified Solutions**  
   - When a statement trivializes complex issues by suggesting absurdly simple solutions or dismissive advice.  
   - Example: "Have you tried not being poor? It is much easier."  
   - This mocks the idea that complex problems have easy fixes.

6. **Use of Rhetorical Questions or Phrases That Imply the Opposite**  
   - Phrases like "What a great transformation" when describing a negative change, or rhetorical questions that imply the opposite of what is stated.  
   - Example: "Removing tax breaks for students will be helpful, right?" when it clearly would not be.

7. **Incongruity Between Subject and Predicate**  
   - Linking unrelated or illogical cause and effect, or mismatched qualities.  
   - Example: "He's over six feet, so he must be wonderful." Height does not logically imply being wonderful.

8. **Dismissive or Mocking Tone Indicators**  
   - Use of dismissive interjections like "Psshh," "Yeah, sure," or "Welp," combined with statements that contradict reality or common sense.  
   - These often signal sarcasm.

---

### How to Apply These Indicators to Difficult Examples

| Difficulty Aspect | What to Look For | Examples from Difficult Cases |
|-------------------|------------------|------------------------------|
| **Contradiction Between Description and Conclusion** | Check if the conclusion contradicts the described facts or events. | - Stressful semester called "fun" <br> - Charity promoter called "terrible person" <br> - Calling a dictator "pragmatic" for naive behavior |

| **Mocking Oversimplified Solutions** | Watch for trivializing complex issues with unrealistic advice. | - "Have you tried not being poor?" <br> - "Just be happy then you won't be depressed" |

### Additional Tips

- **Context Matters:** Sarcasm often relies on shared knowledge or social norms. If a statement contradicts widely accepted facts or norms, it’s likely sarcastic.

- **Look for Irony:** Sarcasm is a form of verbal irony where the intended meaning is opposite to the literal meaning.

- **Check for Implied Criticism:** Sarcasm often serves to criticize or mock indirectly.

- **Beware of Literal vs. Figurative Language:** Sarcastic statements often use literal words to express figurative or opposite meanings.

---

### Summary: Quick Checklist for Difficult Cases

1. Does the statement praise something negative or criticize something positive? → Sarcastic  
2. Is there an obvious exaggeration or absurdity? → Sarcastic  
3. Does the statement contradict common sense or reality? → Sarcastic  
4. Is a complex issue trivialized with an unrealistic solution? → Sarcastic  
5. Is a rhetorical question implying the opposite of the literal meaning? → Sarcastic  
6. Is there a mismatch between cause and effect or subject and predicate? → Sarcastic  
7. Is the tone dismissive or mocking? → Sarcastic  

Use this cheat sheet to analyze the statements carefully and identify sarcasm even without the examples at hand.
