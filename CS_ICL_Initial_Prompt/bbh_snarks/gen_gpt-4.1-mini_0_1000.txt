**Cheat Sheet for Identifying Sarcasm in Statements**

---

### Key Principles for Detecting Sarcasm

1. **Sarcasm often involves saying the opposite of what is true or expected.**  
   - The sarcastic statement usually contradicts reality, common sense, or the positive/negative context established by the other option.

2. **Look for exaggerated praise or criticism that doesn’t fit the context.**  
   - Sarcasm often uses over-the-top positive or negative words ironically.

3. **Sarcasm frequently uses understatement or overstatement to highlight absurdity.**  
   - For example, calling something obviously bad “great” or something obviously good “terrible.”

4. **Sarcasm can be identified by the mismatch between the literal meaning and the implied meaning.**  
   - The literal meaning is positive, but the context or tone implies negativity, or vice versa.

5. **Sarcasm often targets something widely recognized as negative or undesirable but praises it ironically.**

---

### Specific Patterns & Tips for Difficult Examples

#### 1. **Sarcasm via Contradiction of Common Knowledge or Logic**

- When one option states a fact or common truth, and the other option contradicts it in a way that is obviously false or absurd, the false/absurd one is sarcastic.

  - *Example:*  
    - (A) "He's a terrible person, trying to promote a charity stream that has raised millions..." (sarcastic because promoting charity is positive, calling him terrible is ironic)  
    - (B) "He's a generous person..." (not sarcastic)

- *Tip:* Identify which statement contradicts what is generally accepted or logical.

---

#### 2. **Sarcasm via Irony in Tone or Word Choice**

- Sarcasm often uses positive words to describe something negative or vice versa.

  - *Example:*  
    - "What a great transformation" said about someone drinking every day (negative behavior) is sarcastic.  
    - "What a great transformation" about exercising (positive behavior) is not sarcastic.

- *Tip:* Check if the positive/negative adjective fits the behavior or situation described.

---

#### 3. **Sarcasm via Implied Criticism or Mocking**

- Sarcasm can be a subtle way to mock or criticize by pretending to praise.

  - *Example:*  
    - "Such profits being made" when the stock was bought high and sold low (loss) is sarcastic.  
    - "Such profits being made" when the stock was bought low and sold high is not sarcastic.

- *Tip:* Look for praise that is clearly inappropriate or ironic given the facts.

---

#### 4. **Sarcasm via Absurd or Illogical Suggestions**

- When a statement suggests something obviously wrong or absurd as if it were good or normal.

  - *Example:*  
    - "Let's get rid of trams and build carriages instead" (carriages are outdated, so this is sarcastic).  
    - "Let's get rid of trams and build lightrail instead" (logical, not sarcastic).

- *Tip:* Identify if the suggestion is outdated, harmful, or nonsensical but presented as positive.

---

#### 5. **Sarcasm via Contradictory Cause-Effect or Outcome**

- Sarcasm can appear when the cause-effect relationship is reversed or illogical.

  - *Example:*  
    - "I lose money for winning? Sign me up!" (losing money for winning is illogical, so sarcastic).  
    - "I earn money for winning? Sign me up!" (logical, not sarcastic).

- *Tip:* Check if the outcome described contradicts the expected or logical result.

---

#### 6. **Sarcasm via Mocking Exaggeration of Negative Traits**

- Sarcasm can exaggerate negative traits or situations by pretending they are positive.

  - *Example:*  
    - "He's such great relationship material when he's drunk" (usually negative, so sarcastic).  
    - "He's such terrible relationship material when he's drunk" (direct, not sarcastic).

- *Tip:* Identify if the statement praises a known negative behavior.

---

#### 7. **Sarcasm via Ridiculous or Impossible Claims**

- Sarcasm sometimes involves stating something impossible or ridiculous as if it were true.

  - *Example:*  
    - "Impossible, as there's no one on the internet" (obviously false, sarcastic).  
    - "Impossible, as there's no one on the Moon" (true, not sarcastic).

- *Tip:* Evaluate the factual accuracy of the statement.

---

#### 8. **Sarcasm via Contradictory or Absurd Comparisons**

- Sarcasm can be detected when a comparison is made that is obviously wrong or ironic.

  - *Example:*  
    - "Everyone knows salt is the highlight of gourmet sophistication" (salt is common, not gourmet, so sarcastic).  
    - "Everyone knows caviar is the highlight of gourmet sophistication" (true, not sarcastic).

- *Tip:* Check if the comparison elevates something mundane or negative as if it were superior.

---

#### 9. **Sarcasm via Mocking False Praise of Negative Outcomes**

- Sarcasm often praises negative outcomes as if they were positive.

  - *Example:*  
    - "Good thing things aren't more expensive now than they were eight years ago" (when they actually are, sarcastic).  
    - "Good thing things aren't more expensive now than they were eight days ago" (plausible, not sarcastic).

- *Tip:* Look for praise that contradicts known facts or expectations.

---

#### 10. **Sarcasm via Contradictory or Absurd Explanations**

- Sarcasm can be present when an explanation or justification is obviously flawed or ironic.

  - *Example:*  
    - "My laptop didn't work underwater, so it must be Asus's fault" (obviously user error, sarcastic).  
    - "My laptop didn't work underwater, so it must be my fault" (logical, not sarcastic).

- *Tip:* Identify if blame or credit is assigned illogically.

### Final Tips

- **Context matters:** Sarcasm often relies on shared knowledge or common sense. If a statement contradicts what is widely known or expected, it’s likely sarcastic.

- **Look for tone clues:** Words like "Wow," "Yeah, right," "Such a great..." often signal sarcasm when paired with negative or absurd content.

- **Check for exaggeration or understatement:** Sarcasm often uses hyperbole or downplaying to mock.

- **If unsure, ask:** Does the statement make sense literally? If not, it’s probably sarcastic.

---

Use this cheat sheet to analyze the options carefully, focusing on contradictions, irony, exaggeration, and context to identify the sarcastic statement correctly.
