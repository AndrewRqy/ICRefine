## Sarcasm Identification Cheat Sheet

### 1. **Contradiction Between Literal Meaning and Context**
- **Sarcasm often says the opposite of what is meant.**
  - If a statement praises something that is clearly negative (or vice versa), it’s likely sarcastic.
  - Example: “What a great transformation” after describing someone drinking every day.

### 2. **Exaggeration to Absurdity**
- **Sarcasm uses exaggeration to highlight absurdity.**
  - If a statement takes something to an extreme that is obviously untrue or impossible, it’s probably sarcastic.
  - Example: “We should start a petition to ban breathing.”

### 3. **Ironic Praise or Blame**
- **Positive words for negative situations, or negative words for positive situations.**
  - If someone calls a bad thing “wonderful,” “brilliant,” “the best,” etc., or a good thing “terrible,” “horrible,” etc., check for sarcasm.
  - Example: “A wave of hypothermia and drownings will be a great way to start the year.”

### 4. **Mismatch Between Action and Description**
- **The described action and the evaluation don’t logically fit.**
  - If someone does something obviously bad and is called “great,” “dedicated,” “generous,” etc., it’s sarcasm.
  - Example: “He’s such great relationship material when he’s drunk.”

### 5. **Rhetorical Questions with Obvious Answers**
- **Questions that mock the obviousness or stupidity of something.**
  - Example: “How could Government control of the internet be bad?” (when it’s clear there are many concerns).

### 6. **Mocking Stereotypes or Common Beliefs**
- **Sarcasm often mocks stereotypes by pretending to agree with them.**
  - Example: “Because women are totally incapable of doing everyday tasks like pumping gas without a partner taking over and doing it for her.”

### 7. **Absurd Solutions or Suggestions**
- **Suggesting an obviously ridiculous solution to a problem.**
  - Example: “Have you tried not being poor? It is much easier.”

### 8. **Use of “Only,” “Always,” “Never,” “Everyone,” “No one”**
- **Sarcasm often uses absolute terms to exaggerate.**
  - Example: “Only rich people can afford healthy food.”

### 9. **Sarcastic Agreement**
- **Pretending to agree with something obviously wrong.**
  - Example: “You are right when you say that he should have just let them get away for assaulting him.”

### 10. **Contextual Clues**
- **Look for context that makes the literal meaning illogical.**
  - If the context describes a negative situation and the statement is positive (or vice versa), it’s likely sarcastic.
  - Example: “So happy I pay for free shipping with Prime” after listing all the problems with the service.

### 11. **Comparisons That Don’t Make Sense**
- **Comparing things that are not comparable or using mismatched logic.**
  - Example: “Protein supplements are way more expensive than $1 breakfast burritos” (implying burritos are a better deal in a mocking way).

### 12. **“Sure,” “Of course,” “Yeah,” “Right” as Sarcastic Markers**
- **These words at the start of a statement can signal sarcasm, especially if what follows is obviously untrue.**
  - Example: “Yeah, let’s crucify this company for making money, they shouldn’t be doing that.”

---

### **Quick Steps for the Test**
1. **Read the context**: What is the situation? Is it positive or negative?
2. **Check for contradiction**: Does the statement’s tone match the context? If not, suspect sarcasm.
3. **Look for exaggeration or absurdity**: Is the statement over-the-top or impossible?
4. **Spot ironic praise/blame**: Is something bad being called good, or vice versa?
5. **Watch for rhetorical questions or absolute terms**: These often signal sarcasm.
6. **Ask: Would a reasonable person sincerely say this?** If not, it’s probably sarcastic.

---

**Remember:**  
- Sarcasm = saying the opposite, exaggerating, or mocking in a way that is not meant to be taken literally.
- Sincere = matches the context, logical, and straightforward.
