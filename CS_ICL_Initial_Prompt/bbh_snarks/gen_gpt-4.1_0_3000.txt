# Sarcasm Identification Cheat Sheet

## 1. **Contradiction Between Literal Meaning and Context**
- **Sarcasm often says the opposite of what is meant.**
  - If a statement praises something that is clearly negative (or vice versa), it’s likely sarcastic.
  - **Example:** “What a great transformation” after describing someone drinking every day.

## 2. **Exaggeration or Absurdity**
- **Sarcastic statements often use exaggeration to highlight absurdity.**
  - If a statement is so extreme it’s obviously not meant literally, it’s probably sarcastic.
  - **Example:** “We should start a petition to ban breathing.”

## 3. **Ironic Praise or Blame**
- **Sarcasm uses praise for bad things or blame for good things.**
  - If someone is called “brilliant” or “the best” after doing something obviously bad, it’s sarcasm.
  - **Example:** “What a brilliant person” for leaving space junk in orbit.

## 4. **Mocking Common Sense or Reality**
- **Sarcasm mocks what everyone knows to be true.**
  - If a statement pretends not to know something obvious, it’s sarcastic.
  - **Example:** “Impossible, as there’s no one on the internet.”

## 5. **Rhetorical Questions with Obvious Answers**
- **Sarcasm often uses rhetorical questions where the answer is obvious and the question is meant to mock.**
  - **Example:** “How could government control of the internet be bad?” (implying it obviously is bad).

## 6. **Absurd Solutions or Suggestions**
- **Sarcasm proposes ridiculous solutions to serious problems.**
  - **Example:** “Have you tried not being poor? It is much easier.”

## 7. **Mismatch Between Description and Conclusion**
- **Sarcasm describes something negative, then concludes with a positive statement (or vice versa).**
  - **Example:** “It’s been a fun semester” after listing only stressful events.

## 8. **Use of “Totally,” “Clearly,” “Obviously,” “Of course,” etc.**
- **These words often signal sarcasm when used to exaggerate a point.**
  - **Example:** “He only urged; totally different” (when the difference is being downplayed sarcastically).

## 9. **Praise for the Absurd or Unpleasant**
- **Calling something obviously unpleasant “beautiful,” “classy,” “fun,” etc. is usually sarcastic.**
  - **Example:** “Concrete plates. Very beautiful.”

## 10. **Agreement with an Absurd Premise**
- **Sarcasm sometimes agrees with a ridiculous idea to mock it.**
  - **Example:** “You mean Knuth up arrow notation isn’t normal for you?”

---

### **Quick Steps for Each Question:**
1. **Look for contradiction** between the statement and reality/common sense.
2. **Check for exaggeration** or absurdity.
3. **See if praise or blame is being used ironically.**
4. **Ask if the statement is mocking something obvious.**
5. **Watch for rhetorical questions with obvious answers.**
6. **Identify mismatches** between the description and the conclusion.
7. **Notice if the statement proposes an absurd solution.**
8. **Look for sarcastic signal words** (“totally,” “of course,” etc.).
9. **Check if the statement agrees with an obviously bad idea.**

---

### **Common Patterns in Options:**
- **Sarcastic option often uses positive words for negative things, or negative words for positive things.**
- **Sarcastic option often contains an exaggeration or absurdity.**
- **Sarcastic option often mocks or trivializes a serious issue.**
- **Sarcastic option often uses irony to highlight the opposite of what is stated.**

---

**If stuck, ask yourself:**  
*“Is this statement saying something that is obviously not true, or is it using praise/blame in a way that doesn’t fit the situation?”*  
If yes, it’s probably sarcastic.

Question: Which statement is sarcastic?
Options:
(A) You mean Knuth up arrow notation isn't unfamiliar for you?
(B) You mean Knuth up arrow notation isn't normal for you?
Reasoning: Let's think step by step.  
We need to identify which statement is sarcastic. Option (A) says "You mean Knuth up arrow notation isn't unfamiliar for you?" — this suggests surprise that someone is actually familiar with a very obscure mathematical notation, which is a sincere expression of surprise. Option (B) says "You mean Knuth up arrow notation isn't normal for you?" — this implies that it is normal to be familiar with a highly specialized notation, which is an unreasonable expectation and thus sarcastic. The sarcasm comes from pretending that something obscure is actually common knowledge. So the answer is (B).
Answer: (B)
