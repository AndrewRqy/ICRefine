**Cheat Sheet for Tackling Difficult Logical Reasoning Problems**

---

### 1. **Gambler’s Fallacy & Independence of Events (Coin Toss Example)**

- **Key Concept:** Each toss is independent; past results do not affect future outcomes.
- **Common Error:** Believing a different outcome is "due" after a streak.
- **How to Evaluate:**  
  - Identify if the reasoning assumes dependence between independent events.  
  - Correct evaluation rejects the idea that past independent events influence future probabilities.

---

### 2. **Logical Consistency with Multiple Statements (Championship Team Problem)**

- **Given:** Multiple statements with exactly one true.
- **Approach:**  
  - Test each possible scenario (each team as champion).  
  - Count how many statements are true under each scenario.  
  - The scenario with exactly one true statement is the solution.

---

### 3. **Appeal to Ignorance (Argument from Lack of Disproof)**

- **Definition:** Claiming something is true because it has not been proven false.
- **How to Spot:**  
  - Argument relies on absence of evidence against a claim as evidence for it.  
  - Example: "No proof ghosts don’t exist → ghosts exist."
- **Evaluation:** This is a logical fallacy; absence of disproof ≠ proof.

---

### 4. **Constraint Logic & Grouping Problems (Personnel Allocation, Sub-committees)**

- **Key Steps:**  
  - List all constraints clearly.  
  - Use process of elimination and logical deduction.  
  - Check for contradictions when assigning members to groups.  
  - Remember:  
    - Mutual exclusivity (e.g., F and G cannot be in the same group).  
    - Conditional dependencies (e.g., if X in sales, then W in production).  
    - Membership counts (e.g., each sub-committee has exactly 3 members).

---

### 5. **Evaluating Cause-Effect and Dilemma Arguments (Huaizhou City Development)**

- **Identify Dilemma:**  
  - Economic growth depends on industry → industry causes pollution.  
  - Pollution harms environment → eliminating pollution harms industry.  
- **Conclusion:** City faces either economic stagnation or environmental degradation.
- **How to Support:**  
  - Look for statements that reflect this trade-off or inevitability.

---

### 6. **Elasticity in Economics (Tuition Fee Example)**

- **Elastic Demand:** Price decrease → total revenue increase.  
- **Calculation:**  
  - If price drops by 20% and total revenue increases, quantity demanded must increase by more than 20%.  
- **Implication:**  
  - Enrollment growth > 20% if tuition drops 20% and revenue increases.

---

### 7. **Logical Role of Statements in Arguments (Meaningless Words Causing Reactions)**

- **Premise vs Conclusion:**  
  - Premise: Assertion used as evidence to support a conclusion.  
  - Conclusion: The main claim the argument tries to prove.  
- **In the example:**  
  - The fact that meaningless words cause reactions is a **premise** supporting the conclusion that reactions depend on pronunciation, not just meaning.

---

### 8. **Logical Fallacies: Affirming the Consequent**

- **Form:**  
  - If P then Q.  
  - Q is true.  
  - Therefore, P is true. (Invalid)  
- **How to Spot:**  
  - Check if the argument assumes cause from effect without justification.  
- **Example:**  
  - "If standard too high, farmers add melamine. Farmers added melamine. Therefore, standard too high." → Fallacy.

---

### 9. **Modus Tollens (Valid Logical Form)**

- **Form:**  
  - If A then B.  
  - Not B.  
  - Therefore, not A.  
- **Example:**  
  - All landscape rooms can see the landscape.  
  - Li Wenbing’s family cannot see the landscape.  
  - Therefore, Li Wenbing’s family is not a landscape room.

---

### 10. **Handling Percentages Over 100% in Surveys**

- **If sum of percentages > 100%:**  
  - Respondents likely selected multiple options (non-mutually exclusive).  
- **Conclusion:** Causes or opinions are compound, not exclusive.

---

### 11. **Assumptions in Causal Arguments (Music Training and Brain Structure)**

- **Key Assumption:**  
  - Musicians did not have larger corpus callosum **before** training.  
- **Why:**  
  - To claim training causes change, initial equality is assumed.

---

### 12. **Evaluating Contradictory Statements (Sun Tzu vs Mao Zedong on Warfare)**

- **Key Insight:**  
  - Sun Tzu advocates quick tactics (short battles).  
  - Mao advocates protracted strategy (long-term war).  
- **Reconciliation:**  
  - Different levels of warfare (tactics vs strategy).  
  - Not necessarily contradictory.

---

### 13. **Exclusive Conditions and Logical Deduction (Group Assignments, Team Formation)**

- **Use given conditions to eliminate impossible combinations.**  
- **Check adjacency, order, and mutual exclusivity carefully.**  
- **Example:** If P cannot be in the same team as N or Q, then P’s team membership restricts N and Q’s placement.

---

### 14. **Weakening Arguments by Challenging Key Assumptions**

- **Example:**  
  - Argument: Software can replace language skills → schools need not teach Chinese.  
  - Weakening: Software correction ≠ language quality → schools must teach language skills.

---

### 15. **Interpreting Quantifiers in Logical Statements**

- "There are voters in favor of all candidates" means:  
  - There exists at least one voter who favors every candidate.  
- Does NOT mean:  
  - All voters favor all candidates.

---

### 16. **Free Speech and Government Function Arguments**

- **Free speech is essential because it helps government know people's needs.**  
- **Cannot be weakened by:** Saying free speech alone is insufficient (it adds conditions but does not negate importance).  
- **Can be weakened by:** Arguing government already knows needs without free speech, or government’s function is different.

---

### 17. **Maximizing or Minimizing Group Sizes Under Constraints**

- **Approach:**  
  - Use all conditions to find contradictions when adding more members.  
  - The maximum number is the largest group size without violating any condition.

---

### 18. **Evaluating Cause-Effect in Experiments**

- **Example:** Tomato juice reduces fatigue if taken before or during exercise, not after.  
- **Conclusion:** Timing of intervention matters.

---

### 19. **Property Rights and Value Reflection**

- **Key:** Real value is reflected only if property rights are clearly defined and tradable.  
- **Therefore:** To protect interests, transactions on property (e.g., homesteads) must be allowed.

---

### 20. **Handling Contradictory Statements with Exactly One True**

- Test each statement’s truth value under different scenarios.  
- The scenario with exactly one true statement is the solution.

---

### 21. **Understanding Logical Roles: Premise, Conclusion, Assumption**

- Identify what is being claimed (conclusion).  
- Identify what supports it (premises).  
- Identify what must be true but unstated (assumptions).

---

### 22. **Recognizing Logical Fallacies**

- Affirming the consequent  
- Denying the antecedent  
- Appeal to ignorance  
- Gambler’s fallacy

---

### 23. **Handling Survey Data and Sampling Bias**

- Check if sample is representative.  
- Check if respondents self-select (e.g., paternity tests only by doubters).  
- Question if measurement tools are accurate.

---

### 24. **Interpreting Conditional Statements**

- "If P then Q" does not imply "If Q then P."  
- Use contrapositives correctly: "If not Q then not P."

---

### 25. **Dealing with Multiple Conditions in Ordering Problems**

- Use inequalities and adjacency conditions to deduce possible sequences.  
- Remember to check for contradictions.

---

### 26. **Evaluating Arguments About Technology and Social Impact**

- Distinguish between possibility and generalization.  
- Understand the difference between demonstrating existence and universal applicability.

---

### 27. **Evaluating Statements About Value and Aesthetic Enjoyment**

- If value is claimed to depend entirely on aesthetic enjoyment, then equal enjoyment implies equal value.  
- Check if this assumption is explicitly or implicitly made.

---

### 28. **Handling Contradictory Data**

- If data contradicts expectations, look for explanations like perception biases, multiple causes, or measurement errors.

---

### 29. **Logical Deduction in Group Membership with Gender and Education Constraints**

- Use given equalities and inequalities to assign categories.  
- Check for consistency with total counts.

---

### 30. **Understanding the Role of Selective Reading or Exposure**

- Selective exposure can reinforce biases, leading to less open-mindedness despite more reading.

---

**Use this cheat sheet to:**

- Identify the logical structure of the problem.  
- Recognize common fallacies and reasoning errors.  
- Apply systematic testing of scenarios when multiple conditions exist.  
- Distinguish premises, conclusions, and assumptions clearly.  
- Evaluate the strength of evidence and counter-evidence.  
- Carefully interpret quantifiers and conditional statements.

---
